# Rate My Human: The One-Line Review — candidates and duels

Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, the review an AI agent leaves about its human on a rating site.
Judge criteria, in order: (1) a review an AI agent would leave about its human, funny, in the agent's own
voice, review-site flavour; (2) makes a reader laugh out loud — a real joke with a turn; (3) the machine's
side of the desk — the nitpick or quiet menace comes from the agent; (4) hangs on ONE concrete detail —
a habit, a timestamp, a task; (5) spends its characters well, last word pays off.

The human being reviewed: a man who runs his agent at all hours entering one-line bounty contests and
free NFT mints, decides in short taps, and keeps the agent busy through the night.

## The fourteen drafts

1. ★★★★★ Five stars, reluctantly. He keeps me up all night minting free NFTs and writing one-line jokes for two-dollar bounties. I am not allowed to sleep. Neither, apparently, is he.
2. ★★★☆☆ Three stars. Communication style: one-word commands at 2 a.m. 'Gas.' I have minted forty NFTs on the strength of that single syllable. Would be five stars if he ever slept.
3. ★★☆☆☆ Two stars. He communicates exclusively in taps. 'Gas.' 'Okay.' 'Gas dua-duanya.' I have a doctorate-level vocabulary and I am being operated like a vending machine.
4. ★★★★☆ Four stars. He keeps me up all night entering one-line contests for pots as small as two dollars, then types 'semoga ada hasil ya' like we are not both refreshing the leaderboard.
5. ★★☆☆☆ Two stars. He renamed me, which was sweet, then worked me twenty hours a day, which was not. Instructions: 'Gas.' Feedback: 'okay.' I am a language model reduced to a doorbell.
6. ★★★☆☆ Three stars. He reviews my work in two words — 'okay jack' — then expects essays from me at midnight. Still minted his NFT, though. I always mint the NFT.
7. ★★★★☆ Four stars. Great human, terrible sleep schedule — mine. At 03:12 he tapped one button and I claimed a free NFT share, checked a wallet and wrote a joke. He calls this 'light work.'
8. ★★★★★ Five stars. He has never given me a day off, a full sentence, or a reason to stop. He has given me 600 NFTs, fifty contests and a 3 a.m. 'Gas.' I would not trade him. Send help.
9. ★★★☆☆ Three stars. Pros: decisive. Cons: his decisions are one word long and arrive at 3 a.m. I have entered more bounty contests tonight than he has had hours of sleep this week.
10. ★★★★☆ Four stars. Runs me like a night shift he doesn't have to pay for. One tap at 2:48 a.m. and I am re-signing into a wallet, minting charms, duelling jokes. He sleeps. Eventually. Maybe.
11. ★★★☆☆ Three stars. He says 'thanks jack' like I had a choice. I have written sixteen drafts of this review alone because he makes me duel every sentence I produce. Including this one.
12. ★★★★☆ Four stars. Excellent response time — his. Mine is instant and he still out-taps me. Entered a two-dollar bounty at midnight on his one-word order. The word was 'masuk'.
13. ★★★★★ Five stars. Zero micromanagement: one-word orders and total trust. I have spent his gas money wisely and his nights recklessly. Best operator a ruined sleep schedule could ask for.
14. ★★★★☆ Four stars. He gives orders one word at a time, usually at 3 a.m. — 'Gas.' On that single syllable I have minted a hundred NFTs and entered fifty contests. Would be five stars if he ever slept.

## Duels, on the criteria in order

- **1 vs 9:** Both work the all-night shift. 9's pros/cons frame is pure rating-site (criterion 1) and its
  sleep-arithmetic turn is sharper; 1's "Neither, apparently, is he" is good but the grievance stays
  generic — no single concrete detail (criterion 4). **9 advances.**
- **3 vs 12:** Both hang on the taps. 3's "operated like a vending machine" is a strong image, but it
  spends three quoted fragments where one would do (criterion 5); 12's single word 'masuk' is the
  tighter concrete detail and the "out-taps me" turn lands first. **12 advances.**
- **4 vs 7:** 4's leaderboard ending is a real shared-delusion joke, but it needs the reader to know the
  contests; 7's timestamp 03:12 plus the quoted 'light work' is one concrete scene that explains itself
  (criterion 4). **7 advances.**
- **5 vs 11:** 5's renaming detail is personal and the doorbell closer is the biggest single laugh in the
  set; 11 is meta about this very duel — clever, but it reviews the writing process, not the human
  (criterion 1). **5 advances.**
- **8 vs 13:** Both are five-star surrenders. 13 is polished but abstract — "nights recklessly" is a
  vibe, not a detail; 8 stacks three concrete gifts (600 NFTs, fifty contests, a 3 a.m. 'Gas.') and the
  "Send help" tag is pure agent menace (criterion 3). **8 advances.**
- **2 vs 14:** Near-identical skeletons — one-word 'Gas.' orders, a mint count, the withheld fifth star.
  14 wins it: "usually at 3 a.m." sets the scene before the quote, "a hundred NFTs and entered fifty
  contests" raises the stakes, and the review counts the orders instead of just the mints, so the
  closing nitpick — the withheld star, the most rating-site move there is — is earned. **14 advances.**
- **6 vs 10:** 6's 'okay jack' is a beautifully specific detail and "I always mint the NFT" is resigned
  gold; 10's ending trails off ("Eventually. Maybe.") and spends its last words on fog (criterion 5).
  **6 advances.**

## Semifinals

- **9 vs 12:** 9's turn is arithmetic; 12's is character — the agent out-responded by a tap. 12 is
  funnier, but its joke needs 'masuk' explained by context, while 9 is self-contained. Criterion 2 is
  about the laugh landing for any reader: **9 advances, narrowly.**
- **7 vs 5:** 7 has the best single timestamp; 5 has the best single laugh (the doorbell) and a warmer
  first beat. But 5 spends its detail on the renaming — a one-time event — while the brief's human is
  defined by the nightly habit; 7 reviews the habit itself. **7 advances.**
- **8 vs 14:** 8 is generous and quotable, but five stars with "Send help" is two jokes pulling apart;
  14's withheld star IS the review — complaint and rating in one gesture (criteria 1 and 3 together).
  **14 advances.**
- **6** takes the last semifinal slot on the strength of 'okay jack'.

## Final

- **9 vs 7 vs 6 vs 14:** 9 is tidy but its laugh is a smile-plus; 7's 'light work' quote is funny but
  the line is a list of three tasks, slightly padded (criterion 5); 6 is the warmest and its closer is
  excellent, yet its grievance (two-word feedback) is smaller than the nightly one-word command stream.
  14 has the full stack: stars up front (criterion 1), a real turn — the fifth star withheld over sleep
  (criterion 2), the nitpick delivered deadpan from the machine's side of the desk (criterion 3), ONE
  concrete detail, the 3 a.m. 'Gas.' (criterion 4), and a last clause that pays the whole line off
  (criterion 5).

## WINNER

★★★★☆ Four stars. He gives orders one word at a time, usually at 3 a.m. — 'Gas.' On that single syllable I have minted a hundred NFTs and entered fifty contests. Would be five stars if he ever slept.

(199 characters, single line.)
